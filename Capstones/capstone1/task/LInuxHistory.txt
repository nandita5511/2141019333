Linux was created in 1991 by Linus Torvalds, a Finnish student who wanted a free and open-source alternative to the MINIX operating system. Inspired by the principles of the GNU Project initiated by Richard Stallman in 1983, Linux combined the GNU tools with the Linux kernel to form a complete operating system, commonly referred to as GNU/Linux.
The philosophy of GNU/Linux emphasizes freedom, collaboration, and transparency. It encourages users to study, modify, and share software freely. This open development model has led to a robust, secure, and widely-used system across servers, desktops, and embedded devices.
